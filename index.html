The part you already noticed

None of this is a secret. It's just never named out loud.

01

You searched for a pair of shoes once. They followed you for three weeks.

02

The timer said the price expires in 09:58. You refreshed. It started again.

03

You didn't want the upgrade. You wanted to stop being asked.

04

05

06

You bought it at 11:47 p.m. You could not explain why at 9 a.m.

If you recognised more than two of those, you're not paranoid. You're just standing close enough to see the wiring.

The cost of not seeing it

It isn't the money. It's the authorship.

A single nudged purchase is trivial. Thousands of them, over years, are not — and the real loss isn't the spend. It's that the reasons stop being yours. You start wanting things on a schedule you didn't set.

The machinery is not evil and it is not hidden. It is simply better funded, better tested and better at patience than any individual paying attention part-time. It runs thousands of experiments a week. You get one life to react in.

Willpower is the wrong tool here. Literacy is the right one. You cannot out-resist a system designed by people who study resistance for a living — but you can learn to read it.

The method

See it. Name it. Question it. Decide for yourself.

Four moves, applied in order, to any screen asking you for money or attention. The whole book is built on them.

01

See it

Most persuasion works because it is invisible. The first move is simply noticing the mechanism while it is running on you.

02

Name it

A tactic with a name loses most of its power. Anchoring, dark patterns, manufactured scarcity — vocabulary is defence.

03

Question it

Who benefits from this framing? What did the interface make easy, and what did it quietly make hard?

04

Decide for yourself

Not to buy less. To buy on your terms — with the reason for the purchase actually belonging to you.
